2. Overview & Textual Contents

The Apocalypse of Adam is preserved in a unique Sahidic Coptic manuscript discovered at Nag Hammadi in Upper Egypt in 1945:

  • Codex: Cairo, Coptic Museum, Nag Hammadi Codex V, tractate 5 (pp. 64, line 1 – 85, line 32).
  • Facsimile Edition: Robinson, James M., ed. The Facsimile Edition of the Nag Hammadi Codices: Codex V. Leiden: Brill, 1975, pls. 74–96.
  • Date and Context: Copied ca. mid-4th century CE; the Greek original is widely dated between the 1st and 3rd centuries CE. Strikingly, the work lacks explicit Christian terminology or overt references to Jesus Christ, making it central to debates over non-Christian Jewish-Gnostic apocalypticism and Sethian origins.

5. Standard Critical Editions & Studies

  • MacRae, George W. “The Apocalypse of Adam: V, 5: 64,1–85,32.” In Nag Hammadi Codices V,2–5 and VI with Papyrus Berolinensis 8502, 1 and 4, edited by Douglas M. Parrott. Nag Hammadi Studies (NHS) 11. Leiden: Brill, 1979, 151–195.

Standard critical edition of the Coptic text with English translation, critical apparatus, and extensive textual notes.

Comprehensive critical edition with French translation, philological commentary, and thematic introduction.

Editio princeps with German translation and pioneering commentary. (Reviews: J. Daniélou, RSR 54 [1966]: 285–293; K. Rudolph, TLZ 90 [1968]: cols. 359–362; H.-M. Schenke, OLZ 61 [1966]: cols. 32–34).
