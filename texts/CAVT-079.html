2. Overview & Textual Contents

The Book of Jubilees (Liber Iubilaeorum / The Little Genesis / Leptogenesis / Ge'ez: መጽሐፈ ፡ ኩፋሌ Mashafa Kufale ["Book of Divisions"]) is one of the monumental masterworks of Second Temple Jewish literature. Composed in Hebrew between 160 and 150 BCE in Judea (early Maccabean / Hasmonean era) by a priestly scribe aligned with proto-Essenic / Enochic circles, the work rewitnesses and rewrites Genesis 1 through Exodus 12 within a rigid chronological, halakhic, and theological framework:

1. The Sinai Frame Narrative (ch. 1): During Moses’ forty days and nights on Mount Sinai, God commands the Angel of the Presence (Mal'akh ha-Panim) to dictate the primordial history of the world and the eternal laws inscribed upon the "Heavenly Tablets" (Luhot ha-Shamayim).

2. The Sabbatical Jubilee Chronology: Sacred history from the six days of Creation to Israel's arrival at the Jordan is measured into exactly fifty Jubilees of forty-nine years each ($50 \times 49 = 2,450$ years). Every biblical event (the Fall, the Flood, the births and deaths of the patriarchs, the Exodus) is assigned a precise date by Jubilee, week of years ($7$ years), and year within the week.

3. The 364-Day Solar Calendar (chs. 4, 6): The text mounts an uncompromising polemic against the 354-day lunar calendar used by the Hellenistic Seleucid empire and mainstream temple circles. The true divine year comprises exactly 52 weeks (364 days), divided into four quarters of 91 days ($13 \times 7$), ensuring that holy days, Sabbaths, and festivals never drift or fall on unauthorized days.

4. Retrojection of Mosaic Halakhah to the Patriarchs: The commandments of the Torah are depicted as eternal cosmic ordinances practiced in heaven by the angels and revealed to the pre-Sinaitic righteous:

  • Adam observes the purification laws of childbirth (ch. 3);
  • Noah institutes the Feast of Weeks (Shavuot) to commemorate the cosmic covenant and is instructed in medicinal herbs to ward off demonic diseases (chs. 6, 10);
  • Abraham celebrates the Feast of Booths (Sukkot) and the first fruits, practices circumcision, and shuns exogamy and idolatry (chs. 16, 21);
  • Jacob institutes the tithes and the Day of Atonement (chs. 32, 34).

5. Cosmic Dualism, Angels, and Mastema: The cosmos is divided between the holy angels (the Angels of the Presence and Angels of Sanctification, created on the first day) and the demonic realm. After the fall of the Watchers (chs. 4–5) and their imprisonment in the depths of the earth, Prince Mastema (the chief of evil spirits) petitions God to retain one-tenth of the disembodied spirits of the giants to tempt and punish wicked humanity, while nine-tenths are cast into the pit of condemnation (ch. 10).

6. Sacred Geography (chs. 8–9): Noah divides the earth by lot among his three sons (Shem, Ham, Japheth) under solemn oaths. Mount Zion is identified as the cosmic center and "navel of the earth" (omphalos gēs), paired with the Garden of Eden and Mount Sinai as the three holy sanctuaries of God.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

A. Hebrew Dead Sea Scrolls (Original Hebrew Text)

The discovery of the Dead Sea Scrolls confirmed that Hebrew was the original language of Jubilees. Over fifteen fragmentary Hebrew manuscripts were recovered from five caves at Qumran:

  • Cave 1: 1Q17 (1QJub$^a$), 1Q18 (1QJub$^b$) (ed. J. T. Milik, DJD 1, 1955).
  • Cave 2: 2Q19 (2QJub$^a$), 2Q20 (2QJub$^b$) (ed. M. Baillet, DJD 3, 1962).
  • Cave 3: 3Q5 (3QJub) (ed. M. Baillet, DJD 3, 1962).
  • Cave 4: The largest collection, dating from ca. 125 BCE to 50 CE:
  • 4Q216 (4QJub$^a$): Late 2nd cent. BCE (one of the oldest scrolls; chs. 1–2).
  • 4Q217 (4QpapJub$^b$?): Papyrus manuscript.
  • 4Q218 (4QJub$^c$); 4Q219 (4QJub$^d$); 4Q220 (4QJub$^e$); 4Q221 (4QJub$^f$); 4Q222 (4QJub$^g$); 4Q223–224 (4QpapJub$^h$).
  • Cave 11: 11Q12 (11QJub) (ed. F. García Martínez et al., DJD 23, 1998).
  • Canonical Status at Qumran: Cited as an authoritative, inspired text in the Damascus Document (CD 16:2–4: "the Book of the Divisions of the Times into their Jubilees and Weeks").

B. Ethiopic (Ge'ez: Mashafa Kufale)

The only textual witness preserving the entire work complete from beginning to end. Preserved in 19 known manuscripts, classified into two major families:

  • Group 1 (Older, Superior Recension; 15th–16th cent.):
  • MS A (Paris, BnF, MS éth. 51, fols. 1r–55v): 15th century.
  • MS B (Lake Tana, Kebran Gabriel Monastery MS 9): 15th century.
  • Tübingen MSS (collated by Ewald).
  • Group 2 (Later, Secondary Recension; 17th–18th cent.):
  • MS C (London, British Library, MS Add. 16188).
  • MS D (London, British Library, MS Or. 485).
  • Critical Editions: August Dillmann (1859); R. H. Charles (1895); James C. VanderKam (CSCO 510–511, 1989).

C. Latin Tradition (Parva Genesis)

  • Milan, Biblioteca Ambrosiana, MS C. 73 inf. (5th/6th cent. CE uncial palimpsest):
  • Preserves approximately one-third of the text in an ancient Old Latin translation made directly from the Greek Vorlage.
  • Discovered and published by Antonio Maria Ceriani, Monumenta Sacra et Profana 1 (Milan, 1861), pp. 15–54; reedited with exhaustive commentary by Hermann Rönsch (1874).

D. Greek Fragments and External Citations

  • Preserved in extensive excerpts in the Byzantine chronicles of Georgius Syncellus, Georgius Cedrenus, the Chronicon Paschale, and patristic catenae on Genesis; cited also by Epiphanius (Panarion 39.6).
  • Editions: R. H. Charles (1902); Albert-Marie Denis, PVTG 3 (1970).

E. Syriac Fragments

  • Excerpts preserved in London, BL MS Add. 12154 (8th cent. CE), the anonymous Chronicle of 846, and the chronicle of Michael the Syrian (ed. Ceriani, Baumstark).
