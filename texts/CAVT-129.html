2. Overview & Textual Contents

The Apocryphon of Jannes and Jambres (Liber de Ianis et Mambres / Ἰάννης καὶ Ἰαμβρῆς / Poenitentia Iamnis et Mambres) is an ancient Jewish narrative pseudepigraphon composed in Greek in Roman Egypt during the 1st or early 2nd century CE. Expanding upon Exodus 7:11 and the legend of Pharaoh’s court sorcerers who contended with Moses and Aaron, the work tells the dramatic story of the brothers Jannes and Jambres (Mambres), their rivalry with Moses, their fatal humiliation by divine power, and their repentance and post-mortem vision of hell:

1. The Magicians’ Challenge and the Egyptian Plagues: Jannes and Jambres, famed magicians dwelling in Memphis with their aged mother, are summoned to the royal court to counter Moses’ wonders. Although initially able to duplicate the lesser signs through demonic magic, they are stricken by the plagues (boils, darkness), acknowledging the "finger of God."

2. The Fatal Sickness and Death of Jannes: Stricken by a mysterious, incurable wasting disease, Jannes takes to his bed. He opens his secret grimoires and realizes that the divine decree against Egypt is irreversible. Confessing the futility of human sorcery against the God of the Hebrews, Jannes dies in spiritual agony and descends to Hades.

3. The Necromancy of Jambres: Surviving in grief and isolation, Jambres resolves to consult his dead brother. Performing a necromantic ritual with his magical books beside Jannes’ tomb, Jambres summons the shade (eidōlon) of Jannes from the abyss.

4. The Ghost’s Testimony from the Underworld: The shade of Jannes emerges from the underworld and delivers an agonizing monologue. He confirms the absolute justice of God's judgment, describes the terrifying topography of Hades, the relentless torments suffered by the damned, and the inescapable retribution awaiting magicians and idolaters. He implores Jambres to repent and burn their magical books, lamenting that they have forfeited eternal life for fleeting earthly renown.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

Long known only through external patristic citations, New Testament allusions, and a medieval Latin fragment, the actual Greek text was dramatically recovered in the 20th century through major papyrological discoveries:

A. The Greek Papyri

1. Chester Beatty Papyrus XVI (P. Chester Beatty XVI; Dublin, Chester Beatty Library):

  • 4th-century CE Greek papyrus codex, acquired by Sir Alfred Chester Beatty in Egypt ca. 1930.
  • Identified and assembled by Albert Pietersma in the 1970s and 1980s. Preserves approximately 98 fragmentary leaves containing extensive continuous sections of the narrative (Jannes' illness, family laments, death, burial, and Jambres' necromancy).

2. Papyrus Vindobonensis Graecus 29456 + 29828 verso (Vienna, Österreichische Nationalbibliothek):

  • 3rd-century CE Greek papyrus roll from Hermopolis; preserves continuous columns from the earlier portion of the apocryphon on the verso of an astronomical text (edited by Pierre Maraval, 1977; reedited by Pietersma, 1994).

B. The Latin–Old English Bilingual Witness

  • London, British Library, MS Cotton Tiberius B. V, Part 1, fol. 87 (olim 97):
  • 11th-century Anglo-Saxon illustrated miscellany preserving a Latin text with an accompanying interlinear Old English gloss (Poenitentia Iamnis et Mambres).
  • Features a famous colored pen drawing depicting Mambres sitting beside a hell-mouth, opening his magic book to summon the fiery, dragon-headed shade of Jannes from the abyss.
  • Editions: Cockayne (1861); James (1901); Förster (1902); Biggs & Hall (1996).

C. Ancient External Attestations

  • New Testament: 2 Timothy 3:8 ("As Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses, so these men also oppose the truth...").
  • Dead Sea Scrolls: Damascus Document (CD 5:17–19: "Yohana and his brother in their cunning stood up against Moses and Aaron").
  • Pagan Sources: Pliny the Elder (Naturalis Historia 30.11); Apuleius of Madaura (Apologia 90); Numenius of Apamea (apud Eusebius, Praeparatio Evangelica 9.8.1–2).
  • Patristic Testimonies: Origen (Commentarius in Matthaeum 27.9; Contra Celsum 4.51); Ambrosiaster (In 2 Tim 3:8); Decretum Gelasianum (listing the Liber qui appellatur Paenitentia Iamne et Mambre among forbidden apocrypha).
  • Rabbinic & Targumic Literature: b. Menahot 85a (Yohani and Mamre); Exodus Rabbah 9.7; Targum Pseudo-Jonathan on Exod 1:15; 7:11; Num 22:22.
