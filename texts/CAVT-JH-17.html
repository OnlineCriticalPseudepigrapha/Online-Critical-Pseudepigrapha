2. Overview & Textual Contents

The Hellenistic Synagogal Prayers comprise a collection of sixteen Greek liturgical prayers embedded within the late 4th-century Syrian Christian church order known as the Apostolic Constitutions (Constitutiones Apostolorum, compiled ca. 380 CE in Antioch or Syria, likely by an Arianizing redactor traditionally associated with Pseudo-Ignatius). Originally identified by Kaufmann Kohler (1893, 1924) and demonstrated systematically by Wilhelm Bousset (1915) and Erwin R. Goodenough (1935), these texts are Greek-language synagogue prayers composed between the 2nd and 3rd centuries CE (ca. 150–300 CE) in the Hellenistic Jewish Diaspora of Syria, Asia Minor, or Egypt. The compiler of the Apostolic Constitutions incorporated this existing Jewish liturgical manual into his work, overlaying minimal Christian editorial glosses (typically Christological doxological formulas such as "through Jesus Christ our Lord" at the conclusions):

The Corpus of 16 Prayers (as numbered by Fiensy & Darnell, OTP 2):

1. Prayer 1 (Ap. Const. 7.26.1–3): Thanksgiving after meals (Birkat ha-Mazon), blessing God for nourishment, spiritual life, and knowledge.

2. Prayer 2 (Ap. Const. 7.33.2–7): Morning prayer praising God as almighty King, creator of heaven and earth, and savior of the patriarchs.

3. Prayer 3 (Ap. Const. 7.34.1–8): Cosmic hymn celebrating the order of creation, providence, the elements, and celestial bodies.

4. Prayer 4 (Ap. Const. 7.35.1–10): The Hellenistic Synagogal Qedushah / Sanctus, recounting the praise of the angelic hosts (Seraphim and Cherubim chanting Isa 6:3 and Ezek 3:12).

5. Prayer 5 (Ap. Const. 7.36.1–7): Sabbath liturgy celebrating God’s cosmic rest, the revelation of the Torah, and the Sabbath as a perpetual memorial of creation and the Exodus.

6. Prayer 6 (Ap. Const. 7.37.1–5): Sabbath morning blessing thanking God for the renewal of cosmic light (Yotzer Or).

7. Prayer 7 (Ap. Const. 7.38.1–8): Liturgical memorial reviewing the historical succession of righteous biblical heroes from Abel, Seth, Enoch, and Noah to Abraham, Moses, and the judges.

8. Prayer 8 (Ap. Const. 7.39.2–4): Blessing over proselytes / candidates for initiation, praying for their illumination in the knowledge of the one true God and the abandonment of polytheism.

9. Prayer 9 (Ap. Const. 8.5.1–4): Prayer for the ordination / appointment of leaders (adapted for episcopal ordination from Jewish semikhah traditions).

10. Prayer 10 (Ap. Const. 8.6.5–8): Intercessory petition over penitents and catechumens.

11. Prayer 11 (Ap. Const. 8.9.8–9): Congregational petition for the peace of the world, civil rulers, and the community (Birkat ha-Shalom).

12. Prayer 12 (Ap. Const. 8.12.6–27): The Great Anaphora / Eucharistic Prayer, incorporating an extensive Jewish cosmic and historical review of salvation history from the creation of Adam to the conquest of Canaan.

13. Prayer 13 (Ap. Const. 8.15.7–9): Post-communion thanksgiving prayer.

14. Prayer 14 (Ap. Const. 8.16.3): Dedicatory prayer over the offering of first fruits (Bikkurim).

15. Prayer 15 (Ap. Const. 8.40.2–4): Evening thanksgiving prayer accompanying the lighting of the lamps (Lucernarium / parallel to evening synagogue blessings and Havdalah).

16. Prayer 16 (Ap. Const. 8.41.2–5): Commendation / memorial prayer for the deceased righteous, professing unwavering faith in the bodily resurrection of the dead.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

The prayers are preserved as an integral part of the manuscript tradition of the Apostolic Constitutions (preserved in over fifteen Greek codices dating from the 10th to 16th centuries, with early oriental versions in Syriac, Arabic, and Ethiopic):

  • Primary Critical Editions of the Apostolic Constitutions:
  • Metzger, Marcel. Les Constitutions apostoliques. 3 vols. Sources Chrétiennes 320, 329, 336. Paris: Éditions du Cerf, 1985, 1986, 1987. [The standard, authoritative critical text with introduction, French translation, and historical-theological notes].
  • Funk, Franz Xaver. Didascalia et Constitutiones Apostolorum. 2 vols. Paderborn: Ferdinand Schoeningh, 1905; reprinted Turin: Bottega d’Erasmo, 1960. [The classic historical-critical edition].
  • Lagarde, Paul Anton de. Constitutiones apostolicae. Leipzig: Teubner / London: Williams & Norgate, 1862, pp. 239–261.
  • Critical Isolation and Analysis of the Jewish Vorlage:
  • Fiensy, David A. Prayers Alleged to Be Jewish: An Examination of the Constitutiones Apostolorum. Brown Judaic Studies 65. Chico, CA: Scholars Press, 1985. [Redaction-critical monograph establishing objective criteria to isolate the underlying Jewish prayer core from Christian redactional additions].
  • Fiensy, David A. "A Redactional Examination of Prayers Alleged to Be Jewish in the Constitutiones Apostolorum." Ph.D. diss., Duke University, 1980.
