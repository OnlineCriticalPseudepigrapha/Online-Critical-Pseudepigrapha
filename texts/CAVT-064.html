2. Overview & Textual Contents

1 Enoch is a vast composite apocalypse formed of five originally distinct tractates (the "Enochic Pentateuch"), preserved complete only in the classical Ge'ez (Ethiopic) canon of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, but rooted in ancient Aramaic and Greek precursors dating from the 3rd century BCE to the 1st century CE:

1. The Book of the Watchers (1 Enoch 1–36): Early Hellenistic period (3rd cent. BCE). Relates the rebellion of the Watchers (the angels led by Shemihazah and Asael, Gen 6:1–4), Enoch's intercession, and his cosmic journeys through the earth and heavens.

2. The Book of Parables / Similitudes of Enoch (1 Enoch 37–71): Late 1st cent. BCE to 1st cent. CE. Contains three parables centering on the eschatological figures of the Righteous One, Chosen One, and "Son of Man" (identified with Enoch in ch. 71). Absent from the Qumran Aramaic corpus.

3. The Astronomical Book / Book of the Heavenly Luminaries (1 Enoch 72–82): The oldest Enochic composition (late 3rd cent. BCE or earlier). Details the 364-day solar calendar, lunar phases, and cosmic gates revealed by the angel Uriel.

4. The Book of Dream-Visions / Animal Apocalypse (1 Enoch 83–90): Early Maccabean era (ca. 165–160 BCE). Features the vision of the Deluge (chs. 83–84) and the allegorical Animal Apocalypse (chs. 85–90), depicting sacred history from Adam to Judas Maccabeus (the horned ram) and the New Jerusalem.

5. The Epistle of Enoch (1 Enoch 91–108): 2nd cent. BCE. Contains ethical exhortations and eschatological woes against sinners, incorporating the archaic Apocalypse of Weeks (93:1–10; 91:11–17), the birth and wondrous signs of Noah (chs. 106–107), and an appended concluding vision (ch. 108).

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

A. Aramaic Tradition (Dead Sea Scrolls, Qumran Cave 4)

The primary Vorlage of 1 Enoch survived in eleven fragmentary Aramaic manuscripts discovered in Cave 4 at Qumran, copied between the early 2nd century BCE and the late 1st century BCE:

  • 4Q201 (4QEn$^a$ ar): 4Q Enoch$^a$ (early 2nd cent. BCE; Watchers).
  • 4Q202 (4QEn$^b$ ar): Mid-2nd cent. BCE; Watchers.
  • 4Q204 (4QEn$^c$ ar): Late 1st cent. BCE; Watchers, Dream-Visions, Epistle, and Noah fragments.
  • 4Q205 (4QEn$^d$ ar): Mid-1st cent. BCE; Watchers, Dream-Visions, Epistle.
  • 4Q206 (4QEn$^e$ ar): Early 1st cent. BCE; Watchers, Dream-Visions, and Book of Giants.
  • 4Q207 (4QEn$^f$ ar): Mid-2nd cent. BCE; Dream-Visions.
  • 4Q212 (4QEn$^g$ ar): Mid-1st cent. BCE; Epistle of Enoch and Apocalypse of Weeks.
  • Astronomical Manuscripts:
  • 4Q208 (4QEnastr$^a$ ar): Late 3rd or early 2nd cent. BCE (one of the oldest scrolls at Qumran).
  • 4Q209 (4QEnastr$^b$ ar): Early 1st cent. CE.
  • 4Q210 (4QEnastr$^c$ ar): Mid-1st cent. BCE.
  • 4Q211 (4QEnastr$^d$ ar): Mid-1st cent. BCE.
  • Crucial Finding: No manuscript traces of the Parables of Enoch (chs. 37–71) have been found at Qumran; instead, the Cave 4 library preserved multiple copies of the Book of Giants (see §7 below).

B. Greek Tradition

1. Codex Panopolitanus (Akhmim / Gizeh Papyrus; Cairo, Egyptian Museum JE 10759 / P. Cair. 10759):

  • 6th-century CE codex discovered in a Christian monk's tomb at Akhmim in 1886–1887.
  • Contains two distinct copies: GrP$^1$ (1 Enoch 1:1–32:6) and GrP$^2$ (1 Enoch 19:3–21:9).

2. Chester Beatty Papyrus 185 (Chester Beatty XII + Michigan Inv. 5552):

  • 4th-century CE papyrus codex leaves.
  • Preserves 1 Enoch 97:6–104:13 and 106:1–107:3 (lacking ch. 105 and 108), followed directly by Melito of Sardis's Peri Pascha.

3. Papyrus Oxyrhynchus XVII 2069:

  • Late 4th-century CE papyrus fragments containing 1 Enoch 77:7–78:1, 78:8, 85:10–86:2, 87:1–3.

4. Vatican Greek 1809:

  • 11th-century CE tachygraphic parchment manuscript containing 1 Enoch 89:42–49.

5. Georgius Syncellus, Chronographia (ed. Mosshammer 1984):

  • 9th-century Byzantine chronicle citing two extensive extracts: GrS$^1$ (1 Enoch 6:1–9:4) and GrS$^2$ (1 Enoch 8:4–10:14).

6. Qumran Cave 7 Greek Fragments (7Q4, 7Q8, 7Q12):

  • Proposed by E. A. Muro Jr. (1997) and É. Puech (1997) as fragments of Greek 1 Enoch 103:3–4, 7–8, refuting J. O'Callaghan's 1 Timothy hypothesis.

C. Ethiopic (Ge'ez) Tradition

Preserved in over 40 known manuscripts cataloged from the 15th through 19th centuries, classified into two genealogical families:

1. Family $G^1$ (Eth I): The older, unrevised textual recension (15th–16th cent.), exhibiting closest affinity to the Greek Vorlage:

  • Tānāsee 9 (TS): Lake Tana, Kebran Gabriel Monastery MS 9 (15th cent.; complete 1–108).
  • London, BL MS Or. 485 (Lo4 / g): 16th century (base text for Knibb).
  • Berlin, Staatsbibliothek Petermann II, Nachtrag 29 (Be / q): 16th century.
  • Paris, BnF d'Abbadie 55 (Pa4 / u): 15th/16th century.
  • EMML 2080 (Co2): Project of Ethiopian Manuscript Microfilm Library (15th/16th cent.).
  • Cambridge, University Library Add. 1570 (Ca): Dated 1588–1589.

2. Family $G^2$ (Eth II): The vulgate / learned secondary recension (17th–18th cent.), smoothed and harmonized by Ethiopian scribes:

  • Oxford, Bodleian Bruce 74 (Ox2 / b) and Bodleian Or. 531 (Ox1 / a): 18th century.
  • London, BL MS Or. 8822 (Lo1 / d, olim Curzon 55) and BL Add. 24185 (Lo3 / f).
  • Paris, BnF d'Abbadie 35 (Pa3 / t) and BnF éth. 50 (Pa7 / z).

D. Latin, Syriac, and Coptic Fragments

1. Latin:

  • London, BL MS Royal 5 E XIII, fols. 79v–80r (8th cent. CE): Contains 1 Enoch 106:1–18 (the miraculous birth of Noah, discovered and edited by M. R. James, 1893).
  • Pseudo-Cyprian, Ad Novatianum 16.5 (quoting 1 Enoch 1:9).
  • Tertullian (De idololatria 4.2–3, 15.6; De cultu feminarum 1.3, 2.10).

2. Syriac:

  • Chronicle of Michael the Syrian I.4 (ed. Chabot 1899; Brock 1968), preserving an excerpt of 1 Enoch 6:1–7.

3. Coptic:

  • Papyrus fragment from Antinoë (discovered 1937, ed. Donadoni 1960), preserving 1 Enoch 93:3–8 in Sahidic.
  • Related Coptic Enochic fragments (ed. Pearson 1976).
