2. Overview & Textual Contents

The Paenitentia Adae (Penitence of Adam) is a lost work condemned in the Latin Decretum Gelasianum (§ 6, line 297: Liber qui appellatur Paenitentia Adae, apocryphus).

Scholarly consensus (Schürer, Denis, Stone) recognizes that while the original Greek or Latin composition was rejected in early Western decrees, the underlying narrative of Adam and Eve doing penance standing neck-deep in the waters of the Jordan and Tigris rivers became a central block of the extant Greek, Latin, Armenian, and Slavonic Primary Adam Literature.

3. Date, Language & Provenance

Language: Greek (original Vorlage) / Latin ecclesiastical reception.
Date: 2nd–4th century CE; condemned in the 5th/6th-century Gelasian Decree.
