2. Overview & Textual Contents

The Primary Adam Literature encompasses a multifaceted cycle of closely related Jewish and Christian narratives recounting the creation, fall, penance, death, and burial of the protoplasts Adam and Eve. The literature is extant in six principal ancient and medieval versions:

A. Greek Apocalypse of Moses (Apocalypsis Mosis / Greek Life of Adam and Eve [GLAE])

  • Primary Witnesses:
  • A₁: Venice, Biblioteca Nazionale Marciana, cod. Marcianus gr. VII 22 (11th c.).
  • B: Vienna, Österreichische Nationalbibliothek, cod. theol. gr. 237 (13th/14th c.).
  • C: Vienna, ÖNB, cod. theol. gr. 247 (14th c.).
  • D: Milan, Biblioteca Ambrosiana, cod. C 210 inf. (13th c.) — ed. A. M. Ceriani (1868).
  • Additional witnesses: Paris gr. 1313, Paris Suppl. gr. 162.

B. Latin Life of Adam and Eve (Vita Adae et Evae)

  • Classification: Divided by Wilhelm Meyer (1878) into four manuscript classes spanning the 9th to 15th centuries across Continental Europe (especially Munich Bayerische Staatsbibliothek: Clm 4807, Clm 5601, Clm 8470, Clm 13002) and England (12 Insular MSS classified by J. H. Mozley 1928–1929; analyzed by J.-P. Pettorelli 1999).
  • Notable for interpolations such as the Fall of Satan (chs. 12–16) and the quest of Seth for the Oil of Mercy.

C. Armenian Version (The Penitence of Adam / Book of Adam)

  • Discovered in the Library of the Mechitharist Congregation of San Lazzaro (Venice) and the Matenadaran (Yerevan), edited critically by Michael E. Stone (CSCO 429–430, 1981). Closer than the Greek or Latin to the lost archetype in its preservation of the penance in the rivers.

D. Georgian Book of Adam (K‘art‘uli versia)

  • Preserved in 16th/17th-century manuscripts (Tbilisi S-354 and H-1691), critically studied and translated by J.-P. Mahé (1981) and C. Kurtsikidze (1964). Parallels the Armenian text very closely.

E. Slavonic Life of Adam and Eve (Slovo o Adame i Eve)

  • Transmitted in Serbian, Russian, and Bulgarian redactions embedded in Palea collections, edited by V. Jagić (1893), N. S. Tikhonravov (1863), and I. Franko (1896–1910). Includes the related Bohemian (Old Czech) vernacular fragment.

F. Coptic Fragments

  • Sahidic and Fayyumic papyri preserving portions of the Vita and Apocalypse of Moses (Manchester John Rylands Coptic MS 84, ed. W. E. Crum 1909; Berlin Papyrus 181, ed. J. Leipoldt 1904).

5. Standard Critical Editions & Studies

A. Greek (Apocalypse of Moses)

  • Bertrand, Daniel A. La vie grecque d’Adam et d’Ève. Introduction, texte, traduction et commentaire. Recherches Intertestamentaires 1. Paris: Cerf, 1987.

Critical text established from a comprehensive collation of witnesses, with French translation and extensive theological commentary. (Reviews: É. Puech, RB 85 [1988]: 584–585; M. E. Stone, CRBR 2 [1990]: 333–336).

  • von Tischendorf, Constantin. Apocalypses apocryphae Mosis, Esdrae, Pauli, Iohannis, item Mariae dormitio.... Leipzig: Mendelssohn, 1866; repr. Hildesheim, 1966, x–xii, 1–23.

Editio princeps of the Greek text based on MSS A₁, B, C, and D.

  • Nagel, Marcel. “La Vie grecque d’Adam et d’Ève (Apocalypse de Moïse).” 3 vols. Thèse de doctorat d’État, Université de Strasbourg, 1974.

Foundational stemmatic and text-critical study establishing the major groupings of Greek witnesses.

  • Sharpe, John L., III. “Prolegomena to the Establishment of the Critical Text of the Greek Apocalypse of Moses.” Ph.D. diss., Duke University, 1969.
  • Ceriani, Antonio Maria. “Apocalypsis Moysi in medio mutila.” In Monumenta sacra et profana 5. Milan: Bibliotheca Ambrosiana, 1868, 19–24 [MS D].
  • Denis, Albert-Marie. Concordance grecque des pseudépigraphes d’Ancien Testament. Louvain-la-Neuve, 1987, 815–818 [Greek text and concordance according to Nagel].

B. Latin (Vita Adae et Evae)

  • Meyer, Wilhelm. “Vita Adae et Evae.” Abhandlungen der königlich bayerischen Akademie der Wissenschaften, phil.-hist. Klasse 14.3. Munich, 1878, 185–250.

Foundational critical edition based on twelve Continental manuscripts.

  • Mozley, J. H. “The Vitae Adae.” Journal of Theological Studies (JTS) 30 (1928–1929): 121–149; and “A New Text of the Story of the Cross.” JTS 31 (1929–1930): 113–127.

Collations and texts of twelve Insular English manuscripts.

  • Pettorelli, Jean-Pierre. “La Vie latine d’Adam et Eve: Analyse de la tradition manuscrite.” Apocrypha 10 (1999): 195–296.
  • Denis, Albert-Marie. Concordance latine des pseudépigraphes d’Ancien Testament. Corpus Christianorum: Thesaurus Patrum Latinorum, Suppl. Turnhout: Brepols, 1993, 545–552.

C. Armenian, Georgian, and Slavonic Editions

  • Stone, Michael E. The Penitence of Adam. Corpus Scriptorum Christianorum Orientalium (CSCO) 429–430, Scriptores Armeniaci 13–14. Leuven: Peeters, 1981 [Armenian critical text with English translation].

  • Mahé, Jean-Pierre. “Le livre d’Adam géorgien.” In Studies in Gnosticism and Hellenistic Religions Presented to Gilles Quispel, edited by R. van den Broek and M. J. Vermaseren. EPRO 91. Leiden: Brill, 1981, 227–260; and “Notes philologiques sur la version géorgienne de la Vita Adae.” Bedi Kartlisa 41 (1983): 51–66.
  • Jagić, Vatroslav. “Slavische Beiträge zu den biblischen Apocryphen. I: Die altkirchenslavischen Texte des Adamsbuches.” Denkschriften der kaiserlichen Akademie der Wissenschaften in Wien, phil.-hist. Klasse 42.1. Vienna, 1893, 1–104.
