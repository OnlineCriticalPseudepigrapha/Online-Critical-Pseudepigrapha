2. Overview & Textual Contents

Apocalypsis Abrahae (slauice) is indexed in the canonical master concordance as CAVT-089 (CAVT 89). This text belongs to the ABRAHAM - ISAAC - JACOB tradition of ancient Jewish and early Christian literature, predominantly preserved in Slavonic.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

The Apocalypse of Abraham survives entirely in Old Church Slavonic manuscripts copied in Russia after 1300, typically incorporated into the Palea Tolkovaya (Explanatory Palea) or historical-chronographic miscellanies.

As synthesized by H. G. Lunt and classified systematically into five manuscript groups by R. Rubinkiewicz (L'Apocalypse d'Abraham en vieux slave, 1987):

A. Full Text Witnesses (Chapters 1–32)

  • S: Moscow, State Historical Museum (GIM), Sinod. 211 (Sylvester Codex / Uspenskij Sbornik; 14th c.) — the oldest and most important manuscript.
  • D: Moscow, Russian State Library (RGB), MDA 207 (fols. 875–886; 15th c.).
  • A: Saint Petersburg, National Library of Russia (RNB), F.I.433 (fols. 43–64; 15th c.).
  • B: Moscow, GIM, Barsov 634 (fols. 370–385; 16th c.).
  • C: Moscow, RGB, Undol’skij 477 (fols. 317–328; 16th c.).
  • K: Saint Petersburg, RNB, Solovki 699/758 (16th c.).
  • (Additional complete/near-complete copies: MSS H, J).

B. Partial and Fragmentary Witnesses

  • Fragments of the Idolatry Narrative (Chapters 1–8): MSS E, F, G, J, N, R (e.g. MS R: Moscow, RGB, Rumiantsev 358).
  • Russian Abridgments / Epitomes: MSS a, b, c.
  • Reworkings of Fragments: MSS K₀, M, V₁, V₂, W, Y.
  • South Slavic Legends & Echoes: MSS L, M₀, P, T.
