2. Overview & Textual Contents

The Vita Adae (Life of Adam) cited by the 9th-century Byzantine chronographer George Syncellus (Ecloga Chronographica) is a fragmentary chronological witness belonging to the primeval cycle. Syncellus reports that this work recorded the precise sequence and number of days from Adam's creation, the naming of the beasts, and Adam's entry into Paradise, through to the Fall and expulsion.

Scholarly debate focuses on whether Syncellus possessed an independent Greek Jewish apocryphon under the title Vita Adae, or whether he was quoting a Greek text of the Book of Jubilees (specifically Jubilees 3:1–13), which preserves the exact same calendar of primeval events (so William Adler and Michael E. Stone).

3. Date, Language & Provenance

Language: Greek.
Date: Cited ca. 810 CE by Syncellus, reflecting Second Temple traditions dating from the 2nd century BCE to 1st century CE.
Attestation: George Syncellus, Ecloga Chronographica (ed. Dindorf, CSHB, 1829, pp. 8–10; ed. Mosshammer, Teubner, 1984).

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

  • Primary Witness: Georgius Syncellus, Ecloga Chronographica (preserved in MS Parisinus gr. 1711, 11th c., and MS Vaticanus gr. 155, 12th c.).
  • Parallel Tradition: Jubilees 3:1–13; Pseudo-Anastasius of Sinai, In Hexaemeron VII.
