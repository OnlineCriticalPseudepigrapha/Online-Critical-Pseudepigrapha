2. Overview & Textual Contents

The Words of Ahiqar is one of the most ancient and widely dispersed wisdom and narrative cycles of the ancient Near East and Mediterranean world. It comprises two distinct literary components:

1. The Narrative Frame: The biographical account of Ahiqar, wise chancellor (ummânu) and keeper of the signet to Sennacherib and Esarhaddon of Assyria; his betrayal by his adopted nephew/pupil Nadan; his secret preservation by the executioner Nabusumiskun; and his triumphant restoration when he solves the impossible architectural and riddle contests posed by the Pharaoh of Egypt.

2. The Sayings and Fables: An extensive collection of instructional wisdom sayings, proverbs, and beast fables addressed by Ahiqar to Nadan.

The historical reality of Ahiqar's high office was confirmed by the discovery of an 11th-century BCE/Seleucid cuneiform tablet at Uruk-Warka (W 20030,7; ed. J. van Dijk 1962), listing the Assyrian kings and their chief sages: Aba-Enlil-dari, whom the Arameans call Aḫu’qar, serving as sage under King Esarhaddon.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

A. The 5th-Century BCE Aramaic Papyri from Elephantine

  • Witness: Berlin, Staatliche Museen, Papyrus 13446 (found in Elephantine/Yeb, Upper Egypt; dated ca. 425–400 BCE).
  • Editions:
  • Porten, Bezalel, and Ada Yardeni. Textbook of Aramaic Documents from Ancient Egypt (TADAE). Vol. 3: Literature, Accounts, Lists. Jerusalem: Hebrew University / Winona Lake: Eisenbrauns, 1993, Text C1.1 (pp. 24–53).
  • Cowley, Arthur E. Aramaic Papyri of the Fifth Century B.C. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1923; repr. Osnabrück: Zeller, 1967, 204–248 [Transcription, translation, commentary].
  • Sachau, Eduard. Aramäische Papyrus und Ostraka aus einer jüdischen Militärkolonie zu Elephantine. 2 vols. Leipzig: Hinrichs, 1911, 1:147–182, 2: pls. 40–50 [Editio princeps of the Elephantine papyri].
  • Linguistic Analysis:
  • Kottsieper, Ingo. Die Sprache der Ahiqarsprüche. Beihefte zur Zeitschrift für die alttestamentliche Wissenschaft (BZAW) 194. Berlin/New York: de Gruyter, 1990 [The definitive linguistic and dialectal treatment of the Aramaic sayings].
  • Lindenberger, James M. The Aramaic Proverbs of Ahiqar. Johns Hopkins Near Eastern Studies. Baltimore: Johns Hopkins University Press, 1983.

B. Syriac Tradition

  • Extant in West and East Syrian recensions across manuscripts from the 12th to 18th centuries (e.g. Cambridge Univ. Library Add. 2020; London BL Add. 7200, Add. 25875; Sachau syr. 162 and 336).
  • Editions:
  • Harris, J. Rendel, F. C. Conybeare, and Agnes Smith Lewis. The Story of Ahikar from the Aramaic, Syriac, Arabic, Armenian, Ethiopic, Old Turkish, Greek and Slavonic Versions. London: C. J. Clay & Sons, 1898; 2nd enlarged ed., Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1913, 99–127 [Text of Cambridge Add. 2020 with English translation].
  • Nau, François. Histoire et sagesse d’Ahikar l’Assyrien (fils d’Anael, neveu de Tobie). Paris: Letouzey & Ané, 1909 [Syriac texts, French translation, and critical notes].

C. Armenian Tradition

  • Transmitted in two distinct recensions (Recension A and Bodleian MS Arm. g.9 / Venice San Lazzaro MSS), representing an early translation from a Syriac archetype.
  • Editions:
  • Conybeare, F. C. In The Story of Ahikar, 2nd ed. (Cambridge, 1913), 24–85 [Armenian text and translation].
  • Martirosyan, A. A. Patmut‘iwn ew xratk‘ Xikara Imastnoy (The Story and Maxims of Khikar the Wise). 2 vols. Yerevan: Armenian Academy of Sciences, 1969 [Comprehensive Armenian critical edition].

D. Arabic and Karshuni Traditions

  • Circulated widely in the Christian Near East (e.g. Sinai ar. 550; Vatican ar. 450) and entered into the Arabian Nights (One Thousand and One Nights).
  • Editions:
  • Salhani, Antoine. Contes arabes historiques. Beirut: Imprimerie Catholique, 1890 [Early Arabic edition].
  • Smith Lewis, Agnes. In The Story of Ahikar, 2nd ed. (Cambridge, 1913), 130–161.
  • Furaiha, Anis. Ahīkār: Hakīm min aš-šarq al-adnā al-qadīm. American University of Beirut Oriental Series 40. Beirut, 1962.

E. Greek Tradition & The Life of Aesop

F. Slavonic, Romanian, Old Turkish, and Demotic Egyptian Versions

  • Slavonic: Tale of Akir the Wise (Povest’ ob Akire Premudrom), preserved in Russian, Serbian, and Bulgarian manuscripts from the 15th century onward (ed. V. Jagić 1898; A. D. Grigor’ev, Moscow, 1913).
  • Romanian: Povestea lui Arhirie cel Preaintelept, translated from the Slavonic (ed. M. Gaster 1911/1925).
  • Old Turkish (Uighur): Discovered by the Turfan expedition in Central Asia (ed. F. W. K. Müller, APAW, 1908).
  • Demotic Egyptian: Tebtunis and Saqqara papyrus fragments containing translated aphorisms of Ahiqar (ed. K.-T. Zauzich).
