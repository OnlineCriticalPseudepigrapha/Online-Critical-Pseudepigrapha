2. Overview & Textual Contents

The Ladder of Jacob (Scala Jacobi / Old Church Slavonic: Лествица Иакова / Лествица Иаковля) is an early Jewish visionary pseudepigraphon expanding upon Jacob's dream at Bethel (Gen 28:10–22). Composed originally in Greek or a Semitic language (Hebrew/Aramaic) in late 1st-century CE Roman Palestine following the destruction of the Jerusalem Temple in 70 CE, the work is preserved primarily in Old Church Slavonic within the interpretive biblical miscellanies known as the Tolkovaya Paleya (Explanatory Palaia / Palaea Historica) and related florilegia:

1. The Vision of the Ladder and Heavenly Busts (ch. 1): Jacob sets a stone beneath his head at Bethel and dreams of a cosmic ladder of twelve steps extending into the heavens. On each step, he sees a human bust with two faces, while at the summit stands a fiery, transcendent bust resembling God (YHWH). Angels ascend and descend upon the rungs.

2. The Celestial Prayer of Jacob (ch. 2): Awakening in terror, Jacob pours oil upon the stone and recites an extraordinary liturgical hymn of praise to the Almighty God of fire, addressing Him with ancient Semitic mystical names (Sariel, Yahoel, El-El), celebrating God's throne of Cherubim and Seraphim, and begging for the interpretation of the dream.

3. The Descent and Revelation of Archangel Sariel (chs. 3–6): God commissions the archangel Sariel (the heavenly ruler who presides over visionary dreams) to descend to earth. Sariel changes Jacob’s name to Israel and discloses the mystical mystery: Jacob possesses a heavenly counterpart / angelic archetype whose celestial face is eternally engraved upon the Throne of Glory (Kisse ha-Kavod). Sariel then interprets the twelve steps of the ladder as twelve successive world epochs and foreign imperial dynasties. The four most ferocious steps represent the four kingdoms of Danielic prophecy (Babylon, Persia, Greece, and Rome), culminating in the desecration and destruction of the Temple by Rome and the subsequent eschatological judgment of the nations, the downfall of the fourth beast, and the gathering of Israel.

4. The Christian Christological Epilogue (ch. 7): A late antique or Byzantine Christian appendix (interpolated prior to or during the Slavonic transmission, drawing on the "Tale of Aphroditianus" / Religionsgespräch am Hof der Sasaniden) prophesying the miraculous virgin birth of Christ, the appearance of the star of Bethlehem to the Persian Magi, the conversion of the Gentiles, and the overthrow of pagan idols.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

A. Old Church Slavonic Manuscript Tradition

The work is transmitted in three distinct Slavic recensions:

1. Recension A: The fuller, more expansive text:

  • MS S (Sankt-Peterburg, BAN, Solovki coll. 653/712): 15th-century manuscript; chosen by Horace G. Lunt as the base text for the standard English translation.
  • MS R (Moscow, Russian State Library, Rumyantsev coll. 363): 15th/16th century (edited by A. N. Pypin 1862).
  • MS U (Moscow, State Historical Museum, Uvarov coll. 288): 16th century.

2. Recension B: A condensed recension preserving independent archaic readings:

  • MS K (Moscow, Kolomna MS 1406 / Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra): Dated 1406 CE (edited by I. Ia. Porfir'ev 1892).
  • MS M (Sankt-Peterburg, BAN, Solovki coll. 646/921): 15th century (edited by I. Ia. Porfir'ev 1877).
  • MS P (Sankt-Peterburg, Russian National Library, Pogodin coll. 1438): 16th century.
  • MSS V$^1$ and V$^2$ (Vilnius, Public Library MSS 115 and 116): 16th century.

3. Recension C: A very brief, epitomized excerpt contained in the Explanatory Palaia of 1414.

B. Hebrew Cairo Genizah Tradition (CAVT No. 102)

  • MSS Cambridge, University Library, Taylor-Schechter Collection: T-S K 1.144, T-S K 21.95, and T-S K 21.95P:
  • Reuven Leicht (1999) demonstrated that these Cairo Genizah magical/liturgical fragments preserve a medieval Hebrew Vorlage or direct cognate of the "Prayer of Jacob" (Precatio Iacobi) found in chapter 2 of the Slavonic Ladder of Jacob.
  • Editions:
  • Leicht, Reimund. "Qedushah and Prayer to Helios: A New Hebrew Version of an Apocryphal Prayer of Jacob." Jewish Studies Quarterly 6 (1999): 140–176.
  • Schäfer, Peter, and Shaul Shaked. Magische Texte aus der Kairoer Geniza. Band 2. Texte und Studien zum Antiken Judentum 64. Tübingen: Mohr Siebeck, 1997, pp. 27–78.
