2. Overview & Textual Contents

3 Maccabees tells how Ptolemy IV Philopator was prevented from entering the Jerusalem Temple. Back in Egypt, he tries to destroy the Jews by having them trampled by drunken elephants, but they are miraculously delivered.

3. Date, Language & Provenance

It was written in Greek by an Egyptian Jew, probably in the 1st c. BCE, and is preserved in some manuscripts of the Septuagint.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

A. Greek Septuagint Tradition

3 Maccabees was accepted into the Septuagint manuscript tradition of the Eastern Greek Church, though excluded from the Roman Catholic Vulgate canon (relegated in some Latin traditions to an apocryphal appendix).

1. Uncial Codices:

  • Codex Alexandrinus (A / 02): London, British Library, Royal 1 D. V–VIII (5th c.). The primary uncial witness for critical editions.
  • Codex Venetus (V / 23): Venice, Biblioteca Nazionale Marciana, Gr. 1 (8th–9th c.). A critical independent uncial text.
  • (Note: Omitted by Codex Sinaiticus [$\aleph$], which contains 1 and 4 Maccabees, and Codex Vaticanus [B], which omits all books of Maccabees).

2. Minuscule Witnesses:

  • Preserved across dozens of Septuagint minuscules categorized into families by Robert Hanhart (e.g., MSS 46, 55, 58, 62, 71, 74, 98, 120, 130, 243, 311, 771).
  • Sinai Gr. 1342 (fols. 179r–185r): 13th-century minuscule containing 3 Macc 1:1–7:23, identified by W. Baars (1963).

3. Lexicographical and Patristic Citations:

  • Quoted or cataloged in Apostolic Canons 85, the Synopsis Scripturae Sacrae (Pseudo-Athanasius), the Stichometry of Nicephorus, and Byzantine florilegia.

B. Syriac Peshitta Tradition

3 Maccabees is preserved in the Syriac Peshitta Old Testament:

  • Codex Ambrosianus (7a1): Milan, Biblioteca Ambrosiana, MS B. 21 inf. (6th/7th c.), fols. 636–646. Editio princeps by A. M. Ceriani (1876); critical collation by P. A. de Lagarde (1861).
  • Vat. Syr. 461: 16th-century Syriac manuscript cataloged by A. van Lantschoot (1965).
  • Lexicon: W. Strothmann, Wörterverzeichnis der apokryphen-deuterokanonischen Schriften des Alten Testaments in der Peshitta (1988).

C. Armenian Version

  • Fully integrated into the Armenian biblical tradition.
  • Edited by Y. Zōhrapean, Astowacašownč‘k‘ matean Hin ew Nor Ktakaranac‘ (Venice, 1805, pp. 381–388; reprinted by the Mechitharist Fathers, Venice, 1860, pp. 553–563).
  • Studies: H. Bévenot (1934); R. W. Thomson, "The Maccabees in Early Armenian Historiography" (1975).

D. Old Church Slavonic Tradition

  • Preserved in East Slavic biblical collections and the Ostrog Bible (1581).
  • Study: G. Freidhof, "Zur ersten Übersetzung des 3. Buches der Makkabäer im Ostslavischen" (1975).
