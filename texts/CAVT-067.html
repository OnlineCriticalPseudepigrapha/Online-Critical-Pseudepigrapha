2. Overview & Textual Contents

3 Enoch (The Hebrew Apocalypse of Enoch, commonly titled in Jewish mystical manuscripts ספר היכלות [Sefer Hekhalot, "The Book of Palaces"]) is a major late antique Jewish visionary composition belonging to the Hekhalot and Merkabah mystical corpus. Compiled in Byzantine/early Islamic Palestine or Babylonia between the 5th and 7th centuries CE (incorporating earlier Tannaitic traditions from the 2nd–3rd centuries), the text bridges Second Temple apocalypticism and classical Rabbinic esotericism:

1. The Visionary Ascent of Rabbi Ishmael (chs. 1–2): Rabbi Ishmael ben Elisha ascends to the highest heavenly realm through contemplation of the Chariot (Merkabah), arrives at the entrance of the Seventh Heavenly Palace (Hekhal), and is delivered from hostile angels by the supreme archangel Metatron, the "Prince of the Divine Presence" (Sar ha-Panim).

2. The Metamorphosis of Enoch into Metatron (chs. 3–16): Metatron reveals his human origin as Enoch son of Jared, who was snatched from the corrupt generation of the Deluge (Gen 5:24) to serve as a perpetual witness. He describes his bodily transformation into fiery angelic matter, his reception of a celestial robe, a cosmic crown inscribed with the creative divine letters, seventy cosmic names corresponding to the names of God, and his installation on a throne alongside God as the "Lesser YHWH" (יהוה הקטן, YHWH ha-Qatan).

3. The Incident of Aher and the Demotion of Metatron (ch. 16): When the sage Elisha ben Abuyah ("Aher") enters paradise and sees Metatron seated upon a throne receiving celestial homage, he utters the heresy: "There are indeed two powers in heaven!" (Shtei reshuyot ba-shamayim). God orders the angel Anafiel to strike Metatron with sixty lashes of fire (pulsei de-nura) and force him to stand, proving his creaturely subordination (parallel to b. Hagigah 15a).

4. The Celestial Hierarchy, Angelology, and Cosmic Order (chs. 17–40): Detailed taxonomy of the angelic classes (Ophannim, Seraphim, Cherubim, Hayyot ha-Kodesh), their planetary governors, the angelic liturgy (Qedushah), and the river of fire (Nehar di-Nur) issuing from beneath the Throne.

5. Cosmic Eschatology, the Souls of the Righteous, and the Cosmic Curtain (chs. 41–48): Enoch-Metatron shows Rabbi Ishmael the storehouse of souls, the spirits of the unborn, the punishments of Gehenna, and the Pargod (the cosmic curtain hung before the Divine Throne), upon which are woven all generations of world history from creation to the Messianic redemption.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

The textual transmission of 3 Enoch is fluid and complex, preserved as part of larger Hekhalot miscellanies in medieval Ashkenazi, Sephardic, and Italian manuscripts:

A. Primary Hebrew Manuscripts

  • MS B (Oxford, Bodleian Library, MS Opp. Add. 4to 45; Neubauer 1656): 16th-century manuscript; selected by Philip S. Alexander as the base text for his critical English translation in OTP 1.
  • MS A (Vatican, Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, Cod. ebr. 228): 14th century; the oldest dated continuous manuscript witness of the work; transcribed synoptically in Peter Schäfer’s Synopse zur Hekhalot-Literatur (§§1–80).
  • MS E (Munich, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Cod. hebr. 40): 15th-century Ashkenazi manuscript; transcribed by Adolph Jellinek in Bet ha-Midrasch and by Schäfer in the Synopse.
  • MS C (Oxford, Bodleian Library, MS Mich. 175; Neubauer 1657): 16th century; preserves long recensional variants in chs. 15B and 22B–C.
  • MS D (Oxford, Bodleian Library, MS Opp. 241; Neubauer 1653): 16th century.
  • MS F (London, British Library, MS Add. 27199; Margoliouth 751): 16th century.
  • MS G (London, British Library, MS Add. 15299; Margoliouth 752): 15th/16th century.
  • MS K (Cambridge, University Library, Add. 175): 16th century.
  • MS L (Florence, Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana, Plut. 44.13): 15th century.
  • MSS Wert (Jerusalem, collection of Shelomo Aharon Wertheimer): Printed in Batei Midrashot, preserving chs. 23–24 and the distinct Alphabet of Metatron (ch. 48B–D).

B. Cairo Genizah Fragments
