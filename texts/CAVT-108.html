2. Overview & Textual Contents

The History of Joseph (Historia Josephi / Ἱστορία Ἰωσήφ) is a fragmentary Jewish-Hellenistic narrative composed in Greek during the late Hellenistic or early Roman imperial period (ca. 1st cent. BCE to 2nd cent. CE) in Egypt. Expanding upon the biblical Joseph cycle (Gen 37–50), the work relates an uncanonical, embellished account of Joseph's dreams, his sale into Egypt, his endurance in Potiphar's house, his exaltation as vizier, and his filial piety toward Jacob:

  • The Greek Papyrus Fragments:
  • The primary Greek text survives on two sets of 4th/5th-century CE papyrus fragments from the Fayum (now in London and Oxford).
  • The fragments narrate the dialogue of Jacob mourning Joseph, Joseph's prayer while being carried off by the Ishmaelites, and Joseph's refusal to succumb to the enticements of Potiphar's wife, framing Joseph as an exemplar of chastity, divine wisdom, and prophetic foresight.
  • Related Oriental Traditions:
  • The Greek narrative is closely affiliated with extensive late antique and medieval oriental traditions, including the Coptic Narratio Ioseph and the Ethiopic and Arabic Historia Joseph filii Jacob, which preserve elaborated cycles of Joseph's life, death, and assumption.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

A. Greek Papyrus Tradition

  • London, British Library, Papyrus 121 (P. Lond. I 121, frr. A and B):
  • 4th-century CE papyrus fragments from the Fayum; contains portions of the text on both recto and verso.
  • Facsimiles in F. G. Kenyon, Greek Papyri in the British Museum, London, 1893, 1:225, 227; transcriptions in H. J. M. Milne, Catalogue of the Literary Papyri in the British Museum, London, 1927, pp. 127–130.
  • Oxford, Bodleian Library, MS Gr. theol. d. 3 (P) (frr. C, D, and E):
  • 4th-century CE papyrus fragments from the Fayum.
  • Transcriptions by W. M. Lindsay, "The Fayoum Papyri in the Bodleian Library," The Athenaeum No. 3019 (5 Sept 1885): 305.
  • Fragment F:
  • Identified by Seymour de Ricci, "Bulletin papyrologique," Revue des études grecques 15 (1902): 431.
  • Papyrological Repertories:
  • Aland, Kurt. Repertorium der griechischen christlichen Papyri. I. Biblische Papyri. PTS 18. Berlin/New York: de Gruyter, 1976, pp. 391–392.
  • van Haelst, Joseph. Catalogue des papyrus littéraires juifs et chrétiens. Paris: Université de Paris IV, 1976, No. 571.

B. Ethiopic and Arabic Traditions

  • Ethiopic Historia Joseph filii Jacob:
  • Preserved complete in EMML 1939, fols. 124a–168a (late 14th / early 15th cent. manuscript of the Ethiopian Manuscript Microfilm Library).
  • Edition & Translation: Isaac, Ephraim. "The Ethiopic History of Joseph: Translation with Introduction and Notes." Journal for the Study of the Pseudepigrapha 6 (1990): 3–125.
  • Arabic Historia Joseph filii Jacob:
  • Preserved in Arabic Christian manuscripts as an appendix to the Dormitio Joseph filii Jacob (ed. Georg Graf, GCAL 1:205–206; 2:486; F. Manns, Henoch 4 [1982]: 37–40; CAVT No. 116).

C. Coptic Tradition (Narratio Ioseph)

  • Preserved in fragmentary Coptic manuscripts (CAVT No. 111).
  • Editions & Studies:
  • Dochhorn, Jan, and Anders Klostergaard Petersen. "Narratio Ioseph: A Coptic-Joseph Apocryphon." Journal for the Study of Judaism 30 (1999): 431–463.
  • Zandee, Jan. "Iosephus contra Apionem: An Apocryphal Story of Joseph in Coptic." Vigiliae Christianae 15 (1961): 193–213.
  • van der Vliet, Jacques. "Satan's Fall in Coptic Magic." In Ancient Magic and Ritual Power, edited by M. Meyer and P. Mirecki, 401–418. RGRW 129. Leiden: Brill, 1995.
