2. Overview & Textual Contents

The History of Joseph is a retelling of Genesis 37–50. CAVT lists only Arabic and Ethiopic versions, but the work is an original Syriac composition, probably of the early fifth century. The Syriac was translated into Arabic, and the Arabic into both Ethiopic and Latin (Butts, Heal, Moseley and Witztum 2017). Some Arabic manuscripts, like the Syriac, attribute it to Basil of Caesarea. CAVT's further attribution to Ephrem or Jacob of Serugh rests on a Graf reference to a different text.

CAVT 114, an Arabic History of Joseph in a lost Cairo manuscript, is the same text, and the two entries should be combined. The pairing with the Death of Joseph (CAVT 117) found in Ethiopic EMML 1939 already existed in the Arabic.

3. Date, Language & Provenance

Original language: Syriac (early 5th c.).
Versions: Arabic (incl. Garshuni); Ethiopic (from Arabic); Latin (from Arabic, by the Dominican Alphonsus Bonihominis, 1336).
Edition status: the Syriac is edited (Weinberg 1893; Link 1895). An edition of all versions by Butts, Heal, Moseley and Witztum is announced for CCSA (Brepols). The Ethiopic has an English translation only (Isaac 1990); Butts plans to publish his edition of it, perhaps in 2027, and Joseph Witztum is working on the Arabic (Personal communication, A. M. Butts to K. Penner, 28 November 2026.). The Arabic survives in more than thirty manuscripts in several text types, some creatively rewritten (e.g. Paris, ar. 69, 1334, adds Abraham’s rescue from Nimrod’s fire, which the Ethiopic follows); the earliest is Vatican, ar. 175 (1284). EMML 1939 is the only Ethiopic witness (Butts 2020).
