2. Overview & Textual Contents

The Apocalypse of Zephaniah is an ancient Jewish pseudepigraphic revelation recounting the visionary ascent of the biblical prophet Zephaniah through the heavens, his inspection of post-mortem judgment, and his tour of Hades.

Although original Greek copies have perished, the work is directly quoted in Greek by Clement of Alexandria (Stromata 5.11.77.2–3) in the late 2nd century CE:

> "Does not the spirit also speak similarly in the prophet Zephaniah? 'And the spirit took me up and carried me into the fifth heaven, and I beheld angels who are called lords, and their diadem was set upon them in the Holy Spirit, and the throne of each of them was sevenfold brighter than the light of the rising sun, dwelling in temples of praise and hymning the ineffable, most high God.'"

The bulk of the surviving text is preserved in two Coptic dialectal recensions discovered in Upper Egypt at the end of the 19th century:

1. Akhmimic Coptic Manuscript: Preserves 18 leaves recounting Zephaniah's cosmic vision, the recording of human deeds, the confrontation with the demonic accuser, the crossing of the underworld sea in a heavenly boat, and the intercession of the patriarchs.

2. Sahidic Coptic Fragment: A single leaf detailing a vision of cosmic torment and the punishment of hypocrites.

Thematic Structure and Content

  • The Heavenly Books and Recording Angels: Zephaniah beholds the dual bookkeeping of human existence. The angel Dokiel writes down the righteous deeds of humans, while another recording angel transcribes their sins and omissions.
  • The Accuser and the Angel of the Abyss: The prophet encounters the terrifying angel of Hades, Eremiel ("who is over the abyss and Hades"), whose countenance is terrifying and whose hair sweeps the ground, but who functions as a guide. When the demonic Accuser appears to condemn Zephaniah, the prophet prays and puts on the angelic garment, causing the demon to flee.
  • The Golden Boat and the Underworld Lake: Zephaniah is conveyed across an ethereal body of water into the realm of the righteous in a golden boat piloted by thousands of radiant angels.
  • Patriarchal Intercession for the Damned: Zephaniah witnesses Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob daily entreating the Almighty on behalf of the sinners undergoing torment in Hades—constituting one of the earliest witnesses in Jewish literature to the concept of righteous intercession modifying the post-mortem fate of the wicked (paralleling Testament of Abraham and 4 Ezra).
  • The Eschatological Trumpets: Angelic blasts of the trumpet (shofar) inaugurate distinct stages of cosmic reckoning, culminating in the destruction of the world of flesh and the victory of the Lord.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

A. Coptic Manuscripts

1. Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS copt. 135 (Akhmimic):

  • Date: Late 4th or early 5th century CE.
  • Provenance: Discovered at Akhmim (ancient Panopolis), Upper Egypt.
  • Contents: 18 leaves containing the major continuous sections of the Apocalypse of Zephaniah, bound together with the Akhmimic text of the Apocalypse of Elijah.
  • Editions: First transcribed by Urbain Bouriant (1885), critically edited with German translation and glossary by Georg Steindorff (1899).

2. Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS copt. 135, fol. 1 (Sahidic):

  • Date: 4th/5th century CE.
  • Contents: A single folio containing an independent Sahidic version of the visionary ascent and the lake of fire.

3. Naples, Biblioteca Nazionale, Borgia copt. 109:

  • Small supplementary Sahidic fragments closely related to the Paris manuscript.

4. Louvain, Bibliothèque de l'Université, Coptica Lovaniensia MS 12:

  • Date: 5th century CE.
  • Contents: A fragmentary Coptic leaf explicitly preserving the subscript title naming Zephaniah (Tsophanias), published by L. T. Lefort in 1938 (the manuscript itself was unfortunately destroyed during World War II).
