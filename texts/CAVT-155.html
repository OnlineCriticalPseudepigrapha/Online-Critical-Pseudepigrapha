2. Overview & Textual Contents

Eupolemus is one of the most prominent Jewish statesmen and historians of the Hasmonaean era. A member of a priestly aristocratic family in Jerusalem, he was chosen by Judas Maccabaeus in 161 BCE to lead the diplomatic embassy to the Roman Senate to ratify the first official Roman-Jewish treaty of mutual defense (1 Macc 8:17).

In Fragment 5, Eupolemus explicitly synchronizes his historical chronology to the fifth year of Demetrius I Soter (158/157 BCE), establishing that he composed his historical work—titled Περὶ τῶν ἐν τῇ Ἰουδαίᾳ βασιλέων (On the Kings in Judaea)—in Jerusalem during the immediate aftermath of the Maccabean uprising (ca. 158 BCE).

Eupolemus's work represents a nationalistic, apologetic rewriting of 1–2 Kings and 1–2 Chronicles. Writing in Hellenistic Greek with noticeable Semitic idioms, Eupolemus sought to demonstrate that Israel's monarchic history, particularly under David and Solomon, rivaled or surpassed the wealth, imperial reach, and architectural grandeur of the great Hellenistic empires (the Seleucids and Ptolemies).

The Five Surviving Fragments

1. Fragment 1 (Moses the First Sage and the Alphabet):

  • Moses was the "first sage" (prōtos sophos). He invented the alphabet and writing, teaching it to the Jews; the Phoenicians received the alphabet from the Jews, and the Greeks in turn received it from the Phoenicians. (A direct refutation of the Greek claim that Cadmus the Phoenician invented writing).

2. Fragment 2 (David, Solomon, and the Temple):

  • The centerpiece of Eupolemus's historiography:
  • David's Empire: Extends David's conquests across Syria, Phoenicia, the Euphrates, and Arabia. God restrains David from building the temple because he is a "man of blood," commanding him to leave the task to his son Solomon, who ascends the throne at age twelve.
  • The Diplomatic Correspondence: Eupolemus fabricates formal Hellenistic royal letters exchanged between King Solomon, King Vaphris of Egypt (who dispatches 80,000 Egyptian laborers), and King Souron (Hiram) of Tyre (who sends 120,000 Tyrian craftsmen).
  • The Colossal Temple: Exaggerates the physical scale, gold plating, and bronze cast work of the Jerusalem sanctuary, turning it into the architectural wonder of the ancient world.

3. Fragment 3 (The Golden Shield):

  • Solomon dedicates a monumental golden shield in the sanctuary of the temple.

4. Fragment 4 (The Babylonian Exile):

  • King Jonachim (Jehoiakim) commits apostasy; the prophet Jeremiah warns of imminent doom. Nebuchadnezzar captures Jerusalem, carrying away the temple vessels, while Jeremiah preserves the Ark of the Covenant.

5. Fragment 5 (Chronological Eras):

  • Calculates that from Adam to the fifth year of Demetrius (158 BCE) was exactly 5,149 years; and from the Exodus from Egypt to Demetrius was 2,580 years.
