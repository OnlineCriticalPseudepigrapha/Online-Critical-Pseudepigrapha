2. Overview & Textual Contents

4 Baruch (Paraleipomena Jeremiou) tells of the fall of Jerusalem and the exile. Abimelech sleeps for sixty-six years and wakes to find his figs still fresh; Jeremiah leads the return; and the work ends with Jeremiah's death at the hands of the people.

3. Date, Language & Provenance

Most date it in the early 2nd c. CE, after 70 and before the Bar Kokhba revolt. It is a Jewish work with a Christian ending. It survives in Greek, Ethiopic, Armenian and Slavonic.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

A. The Greek Tradition (Paraleipomena Jeremiou)

  • Extant in more than twenty manuscripts dating from the 11th to 16th centuries (frequently incorporated into menologia and hagiographical collections for the feast of Jeremiah on May 1):
  • M: Venice, Biblioteca Nazionale Marciana, cod. Marcianus gr. II.41 (11th c.) — best manuscript witness.
  • V: Rome, Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, MS Vat. gr. 760 (11th c.).
  • P: Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS gr. 1579 (dated 1528 CE).
  • Q: Paris, BnF, MS gr. 1582 (14th c.).
  • H: Jerusalem, Greek Patriarchal Library, Saba 259 (11th c.).
  • Oxford, Bodleian Library, MS Barocci 145 (15th c.).

B. The Ethiopic Tradition (Rest of the Words of Baruch)

  • Widely preserved across the Ge’ez biblical and liturgical corpus as part of the broader Jeremiah-Baruch collection (e.g. Frankfurt MS 18; London BL Orient. 503; Paris d'Abbadie 68). Edited critically by August Dillmann (1866).

C. Slavonic, Romanian, and Armenian Traditions

  • Slavonic: Preserved in Serbian, Bulgarian, and Russian versions under the title Povest’ o plenenii Ierusalima (Story of the Captivity of Jerusalem), ed. I. Franko (1896), V. Jagić (1893), and S. Novaković.
  • Romanian: Translated from the Slavonic (ed. M. Gaster 1925/1928).
  • Armenian: Preserved in historical chronicles and biblical florilegia (ed. S. Yovsēp‘ianc‘ 1896; J. Issaverdens 1901).
