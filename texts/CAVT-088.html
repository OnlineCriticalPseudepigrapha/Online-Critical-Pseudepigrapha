2. Overview & Textual Contents

In the Testament of Abraham, Abraham refuses to surrender his soul to the archangel Michael. He is taken on a chariot tour of the inhabited world and sees the judgment of souls. In the end Death comes for him in disguise and takes him by deceit.

3. Date, Language & Provenance

The work survives in Greek in a longer (A) and a shorter (B) recension, and in Coptic, Arabic, Ethiopic, Slavonic and Romanian versions. It is usually considered Jewish, from Egypt, around the 1st–2nd c. CE.

4. Manuscript Witnesses & Shelfmarks

The Testament of Abraham is preserved primarily in Greek in two distinct recensions:

  • Recension A (Longer): A fuller, more elaborate narrative focusing on Abraham’s heavenly journey, the judgment of souls, and his refusal to yield his soul to Death.
  • Recension B (Shorter): A concise, more colloquial redaction with distinct apocalyptic and folkloric features.

Primary Greek Manuscripts

  • Recension A:
  • A: Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS gr. 770 (dated 1304 CE).
  • B: Jerusalem, Greek Patriarchal Library, Saba 259 (11th c.).
  • C: Paris, BnF, MS gr. 1556 (15th c.).
  • D: Oxford, Bodleian Library, MS Canonici Greek 19 (15th c.).
  • Additional witnesses: Paris gr. 1313 (15th c.), Paris gr. 1613 (15th c.), Paris Suppl. gr. 162; Vienna theol. gr. 237.
  • Recension B:
  • E: Paris, BnF, MS gr. 1613 (15th c.).
  • F: Paris, BnF, MS gr. 1313 (15th c.).
  • M: Milan, Biblioteca Ambrosiana, MS gr. 405 (C 210 inf.; 13th c.) — collated by F. Schmidt.
  • Rome, Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, MS Vat. gr. 1801 (15th c.).

Oriental and Slavonic Versions

  • Coptic:
  • Bohairic version: Rome, Vat. copt. 61, fols. 164v–189v (dated 961/2 CE), ed. I. Guidi (1900).
  • Sahidic fragments: Papyrus Köln Inv. Nr. 3221, noted by C. Römer, H. J. Thissen, and M. Weber.
  • Ethiopic (Ge’ez):
  • Falasha (Beta Israel) and Christian recensions: A. Z. Aešcoly (1951); M. Gaguine (1965).
  • Arabic:
  • Extant in multiple Karshuni and Christian Arabic collections (e.g. Dayr Anba Maqar; ed. W. E. Barnes 1892; catalogue by G. Graf 1944).
  • Church Slavonic and Romanian:

  • Ukrainian redaction (Slovo o sviatom Avraame).

Schmidt reports the existence of five more mss which he was unable to obtain in preparing his text, at least three of which seem to be mss of recension A (see p. 3). Given the extremely wide variation among the surviving manuscripts, even within each recension, Schmidt declined to reconstruct an eclectic text. Instead, for each textual family he prints in running form the text of one manuscript, providing an apparatus which lists all variants in the other mss of the family (only purely orthographic variants are excluded). Schmidt also sub-divides recension B into two ms families, providing separate texts for each family (mss EACDHI and mss BFG respectively). Schmidt's two texts of the short recension employ mss E and B respectively for their running texts and both differ substantially from James' text of the short recension. In his text of the long recension Schmidt employs ms A as his running text. Since this is the same ms which was employed by James as the basis of his eclectic text, Schmidt's running text of the long recension is much closer to that of James than is the case in the short recension. Schmidt retains the chapter divisions of James. In recension B he employs the verse divisions of P. Riessler, while the verse division in recension A are Schmidt's own work.

For further bibliography, see DiTommaso, Bibliography, 565-74.
